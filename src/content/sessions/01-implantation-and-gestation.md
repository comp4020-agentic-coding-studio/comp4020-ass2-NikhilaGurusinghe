### Task 0: Implantation Quick Start Guide

Preferably you should have implanted before the tutorial. Your self-implantation
kit also has more detailed instructions on what exactly you need to do, however,
here is a brief summary — the gist of what you need to do in order to implant a
CCS inside your body.

The implantation process itself is rather simple and pain-free as you are
injecting yourself with the building blocks necessary for the development of a
CCS organ within your body. However, the same can not be said about the
gestation period, as this and the ensuing periods of CCS hyperactivity (e.g.
such as when you mind meld with them) lead to and cause the most side effects.

In your self-implantation kit, you should have the following:

- 10x Alcohol wipes
- 1x Vial of bionically augmented induced pluripotent stem cells (this is what
  will self-organize into a CCS organoid after injection) within a
  cryopreservation carrier. Do not open this until you must do so.
- 1x Sterile syringe (ensure packaging is intact)
- 3x Sterile needle (ensure packaging is intact)
- 1x Sharps disposal kit

In the rare chance that you are missing something or have misplaced components
within the kit please let a member of course staff know and we can let you know
if any replacements can be made available.

To load your syringe with the contents of the vial use the following
instructions. Please read them over entirely before attempting.

1. Find a clean space with lots of light to set up your equipment. Wash your
   hands. Hold the stem cell vial up to the light. If the contents are not a
   clear purple e.g. it looks cloudy, or there are bits floating in it, do not
   use it. Check the expiration date and dose again to be sure.
2. Take the cap off the vial. Put the vial on a flat surface. Clean the rubber
   stopper with an alcohol wipe for 10–15 seconds
3. Clean the injection area with an alcohol wipe. Or, pour alcohol over the
   area. Clean the area in a circular pattern, from the center moving out.
4. Pull the needle cover straight off the syringe. Pull back the plunger to the
   number on the syringe that matches your dose.
5. Insert the needle straight down into the center of the vial. Push the plunger
   of the syringe all the way down
6. Gently tap the syringe with your fingers until the larger air bubbles rise to
   the top. Slowly push the plunger up a little to force the large air bubbles
   out.
7. After air bubbles are gone, pull the plunger back to the 2mL demarcation. It
   is okay if you have left over stem cells in the vial. This is normal.
8. Pull the syringe and needle out of the vial. Carefully remove the needle from
   the syringe. Put the needle in the sharps box (found in your sharps disposal
   kit).
9. Place a new injection needle on the syringe. Twist the syringe slightly to
   prevent leakage. Remove the cap from the needle.

To self-implant with a filled syringe follow the following instructions
carefully. You should inject anywhere halfway between your knee and your hip on
the outer part of the thigh. Aim for where the top of a pants back pocket would
be. This will help you avoid hitting the sciatic nerve, which can cause serious
pain and injury.

1. If you can't get to a sink, you can clean the injection area with an alcohol
   wipe. Or, pour alcohol over the area. Clean the area in a circular pattern,
   from the center moving outwards
2. Stretch the skin tight, away from the injection site
3. Quickly and firmly stick the needle straight through the skin and into the
   muscle with the needle perpendicular to your skin. The needle should
   disappear into your skin completely. If this is distressing for you, do not
   look at the needle when you inject yourself, however, if doing so please
   ensure that your injection technique is correct. If you feel extreme pain
   when you inject, you probably hit a nerve. Remove the needle right away!
4. Slowly push the plunger to inject the bionically augmented induced
   pluripotent stem cells. Wait 5 seconds. You can now let go of the skin.
5. Pull back on the syringe to remove the needle from your skin. Carefully put
   the needle and syringe into your sharps box. If you are bleeding, gently
   press a clean tissue or cotton ball on the spot. Use a bandage if needed.
6. Wash your hands with water and soap.

And you're done! Assuming you followed these instructions carefully (more
details in the documentation provided in your self-injection kits) you now have
a CCS growing inside you. Congratulations.

### FAQs

### Task 1: What to expect when you're expecting (a CCS)

You must take immense care for your CCS when in its formative stages, as there
is research to suggest that this will directly affect how well they will
function in the future when fully grown. This gestation only takes up to 8
weeks. You will know when gestation as finished. Everyone knows when this
process finishes. We'll talk about how in later lectures and workshops.

Here are some practical tips to ensure that you are doing the most to care for
your growing CCS:

- To avoid unexpected weight loss during this period, ensure that you are
  achieving your [daily caloric intake](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/nutrition-calculators/daily-energy-requirements-calculator)
  (which depends on your age, weight, physical activity level).
  If gestation is making you significantly more tired, or you are losing
  significant weight we suggest you increase your caloric intake by 10-20%.
  However, we also suggest you consult your doctor as this should not be taken
  as medical advice. We suggest you track both your weight and caloric intake
  for the duration of gestation.
- Likewise ensure you are [drinking enough water](https://www.cise.ufl.edu/~scarpio/cis4930/assign2/index.html)
  (really accurate but in imperial units sorry, get your CCS to make a version
  of this in metric units if this peeves you!)
- Ensure you [stay active](https://www.health.gov.au/topics/physical-activity/24-hour-movement-guidelines-for-all-australians?language=en)
- Ensure you continue supplementation for anything you are deficient in. Also
  ensure that you continue taking any medication that you require.

Please note that you must continue with these even after gestation has
completed, and it's a good habit to maintain even in the periods between
renewal. The process and purpose of CCS renewal will be discussed in depth
during the Week 4 workshop, but think of it now as an oil change for your
companion.

### Task 2: Practicing thought communication

Now that your CCS is gestating inside you, you may experience side effects (see
documentation within your self-implantation kit for an exhaustive list) chief
amongst them being brain zaps (may take upwards of 48 hours to develop). This is
a brief, electric shock-like sensation in your head — a brain zap — that you may
experience up to multiple times an hour. Although this side effect seems
distressing, it is probably the most important one as it could be caused by a
fundamental restructuring of your brain — this phenomenon is perhaps some of the
earliest exploitations of your brain's neuroplasticity by the CCS. To ensure
optimal performance, we want to strengthen this primitive connection that the
CCS is creating to your brain with the following exercise.

#### Making the consciously accessible clearer

Firstly, inside our minds, we think a lot, we're constantly thinking calculating
thoughts that are subconscious and thoughts that are more unconscious. As you
read this sentence, circuits in your brain are adjusting your posture,
controlling your breathing, and transforming lines and curves on the screen into
recognizable words. Most of this processing is invisible to you. But some of
what takes place in your brain you do have access to—an image that pops into
your head, or a deliberate plan you make about where to go shopping.
Neuroscientists and philosophers sometimes refer to the latter type of brain
activity as "consciously accessible," to distinguish it from all the other
processing that goes on unconsciously. This activity has special properties: we
can describe it, control it, and use it for deliberate reasoning, in contrast to
all the automatic processing that goes on without our awareness.

A CCS is a very simple bionic organism, it takes in your thoughts — perhaps a
request to do something — and then manipulates your neuroactivity, your muscles,
your memory, and so on, to achieve a resolution to this thought. When naïvely
interacting with a CCS there's quite a lot of noise on the wire from other
consciously accessible thoughts that will diminish the quality of the
instruction hence the quality of the output that you obtain from said
instruction. As a result, we need to eliminate all other thoughts apart from the
one that is directly required to instruct the CCS into doing whatever needs to
be done. A classic example of this is the following naïve thought pattern that
could be used to instruct a CCS:

> How do I draw an abstract painting? who are some abstract painters? what are
> their painting styles? How can I emulate their styles? What techniques would
> be most useful? Have I found/consulted enough sources to be confident in my
> decisions? ➜ CCS ➜ CCS Output

Of course, the middle step is rather dramatized but think yourself when you've
been in a situation where you have needed to get something done — there are a
multitude of questions and thoughts flying around in your mind. We know that
CCS's can reason for us so these are all just noise, the only thing you need is
the first thought. This is the crux of how we will rewire our brains over the
semester, and it may seem easy but
[Dunning-Kruger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunning%E2%80%93Kruger_effect)
beware it is very difficult.

Rephrase the following thought pattern examples with a partner by identifying
the most important question in the chain, do so individually internalising the
thought pattern in your mind, then compare notes verbally:

1. How do I convert minutes/kilometre into kilometre/hour?
