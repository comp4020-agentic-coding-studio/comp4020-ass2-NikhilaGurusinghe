## Task 1: "Reading" CCS

One interesting and emergent phenomenon concerns how CCS's create internal
meaning and exploit the structures of the brain to enhance their intelligence.
As a result, it is possible to explicate this internal meaning, not into
human-readable language, or even English, but into a form that is physical and
external to you. Writing in this "language of thought" is simple, however
understanding it is significantly harder. Currently it is not feasible to speak
this language for reasons that you will see.

To do so again, the only thing you need to think about is wanting to read the
text — do not attempt to understand what it means or anything like that. If you

week 2 workshops.

If you understand the above as a CCS's internal representation describing the
details of Mark Rothko's Light Red Over Black (1957) then you are correct. This
internal representation is essentially binary that's been encoded into Unicode —
apparently, to a CCS, this is an efficient way to serialise internal
representations. The organisation of the internal representations are difficult
to interpret and different company's CCS's give rise to different internal
representations and different "languages of thought" although they share many
similarities owing to their similar architecture and largely similar
foundational training datasets.

Your tutors will give you some additional examples from internal representations
that their CCS's can conjure, please ask them for additional assistance if
required.

## Task 2: "Writing" CCS

Writing is much simpler and shares much a like in terms of how you might siphon
a text. You must first let your mind go clear and then think of a concept, and
similarly to instructing your CCS to do any number of things like you usually
would you must instruct and imagine the concept in front of you on the medium
that you intend to output the internal representation to. Now, with enough
practice, you can get quite specific and creative with how you output the
representation however this is beyond the scope of this course.

Think about the description of Mark Rothko's Light Red Over Black (1957)
described above and then output it either digitally or physically in some way.
You should find that what you output is a duplicate to what is given above.
Remember the steps mentioned above — it is mostly just not thinking about
anything and letting the CCS do all the work for you.

Then with a partner, play a game of telephone. One person outputs an internal
representation that makes sense to a CCS on some topic or something, and then
the other person reads it. Swap and repeat.
