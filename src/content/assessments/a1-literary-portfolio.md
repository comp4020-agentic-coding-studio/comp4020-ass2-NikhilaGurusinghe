You will create a portfolio containing:

- 3 rough draft poems, or 1 draft short story or memoir
- 3 poems revised from draft form, or 1 revised short story or memoir
- A rough draft of a screenplay or playscript
- A revised version of the same screenplay or playscript (can be a polished
  excerpt from a longer work)
- Include a summary (1-2 pages total) of the changes you've made to the pieces
  in your portfolio and the rationale behind your changes

Up to 5000-word limit total (applied to the size of your draft poems combined
or short story or memoir) and then an additional 5000-word limit total (applied
to the size of the screenplay or playscript). Ensure you follow formatting
guidelines for the text that you are presenting (use Courier New for plays and
screenplays, or 12 point font and 1.5 spacing for prose etc.)

Additional workshop exercises may be included but are not compulsory.

## Marking Rubric

| Criterion                                                            | Weighting |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| Understanding of CCS communication techniques and utilisation of them | 68%       |
| Portfolio                                                              | 2%        |
| Documentation of CCS utilisation                                       | 20%       |

Your understanding of CCS techniques and utilisation of them (and by extension
documentation of which) is really what we care about — hence it carries the
biggest mark. You can literally not submit any piece of prose, and you’ll be
alright as long as you can articulate that you can mind-meld with your CCS
well. However, you must respond to what is specified and required by the
portfolio as the whole point of this course is for you steer your CCS towards
something and output a high-quality output.

The band descriptors — what HD, D–Cr, P and N mean for each criterion — are the
same for both assignments.

Read the [policies](/policies/) of this course for information on submission,
extensions, special consideration, and appeals.
